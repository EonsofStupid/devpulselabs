## 1. The problem

**The operator's words (2026-07-21):**

> "we want the wiredfront editor to be established for scale and actually moving towards being able to stitching video clips together generated by ai and be able to comfyui workflow them for edit"

Three requirements: **scale** (an architecture that survives many features), **stitching AI-generated clips**, and **ComfyUI workflows as an editing operation**.

### 1.1 What exists today [GROUND]

| Capability | State |

**The honest summary:** the expensive, fiddly part — shipping and resolving ffmpeg — is already done and proven. Everything above it is greenfield.

## 2. What the market actually settled on [RESEARCH]

### 2.1 The volatility fact that shapes everything

**OpenAI shut Sora down.** Consumer app dark 2026-04-26; Videos API and all `sora-2*` models terminate 2026-09-24 with no listed replacement. Reported drivers: ~$1M/day compute against ~$2.1M lifetime IAP.

**Consequence, and it is a hard one:** *never hard-wire a generation vendor.* A backend that looks permanent can be gone inside a quarter. Generation backends are **pluggable adapters from commit one**, and the local ComfyUI path is strategically valuable precisely because it cannot be switched off by someone else.

### 2.2 The interaction model that won

Surveying Runway (Gen-4.5/Aleph), Google Flow (Veo 3.1), Kling 3.0, Higgsfield, Luma Ray3, Pika, Descript, CapCut, Premiere Pro 26, Resolve 21, Kapwing, LTX Studio, and the node-graph tier (Krea Nodes, Flora, Figma Weave):

**A hybrid won, unambiguously — shot-list as the project structure, a deliberately reduced timeline as the assembly surface.**

- **Node graphs lost for assembly.** Krea/Flora/Weave are excellent at *producing* a clip and stop there. No serious tool assembles a sequence in a node graph.
- **Pure chat lost.** Descript's Underlord is the strongest agentic case and it still operates *on a timeline*. Natural language is an accelerator over a visual model, never a replacement.
- **The timeline won as the assembly surface** — Runway, Flow's Scenebuilder, LTX Studio, Kapwing all converged. But it is *reduced*: single video track, trim, reorder, basic transitions, one audio bed. Not a 24-track NLE.
- **The shot-list won as the project structure.** The screenplay/scene list is canonical; clips are generated from a scene and indexed back to it.

**Premiere Pro 26 directly validates the target architecture:** a pro editor as host, with third-party models (Luma, Runway) callable *inside the timeline*. Host owns assembly; generation is a pluggable service.

### 2.3 The economic fact that defines the UX

Practitioners report **20–50 variants per shot**. Reviews consistently flag an "iteration tax": budget 25–50% more credits than expected output, no discount for fixing the model's own mistakes.

> **[INFER, and the most important inference in this document]:** for AI-generated source material, this is fundamentally a **cost- and iteration-management UX**, not a cutting UX. The product is whatever makes a re-roll cheap, targeted, and comparable. Any design decision that makes "regenerate just this shot and compare it against the last three attempts, in context" harder is wrong, regardless of how conventional it looks.
>
> This applies with less force here than to a credit-metered SaaS — local ComfyUI costs GPU-minutes, not dollars. But minutes are still the scarce resource, and the structural argument (never lose a variant, never re-roll blind) holds identically.

### 2.4 Re-generating one shot inside a sequence

Three patterns, increasing sophistication:

1. **Slot-based replacement** (LTX Studio, Flow) — the shot is a *slot* with an identity; generations are candidates that fill it. Regenerating swaps the occupant; neighbours untouched. **The mainstream pattern.**
2. **In-place segment retake** (LTX-2 Retake) — regenerate a *sub-range* of a clip (2–16s), preserving motion, camera flow, ambient sound, lip sync. The current frontier.
3. **Context-aware whole-clip edit** (Runway Aleph, Luma Modify) — transform by prompt rather than regenerate.

**The architectural consequence is the single most valuable finding in the research:**

> A timeline clip must reference a **shot slot**, and the slot owns an ordered set of generation attempts. **Never let the timeline point directly at a file path** — otherwise "swap this shot" is destructive and variant history is lost.

Pattern 2 argues a clip must be addressable by **sub-range**, which the §4 model provides via `sourceStart`/`sourceDuration` from day one.

### 2.5 Bake-off / variant comparison

The accepted pattern is **branching, not overwrite** — documented as a named pattern (Shape of AI, "Regenerate"): overwrite mode keeps only the latest; **branching mode, "common in video and text editors,"** retains each retry as a separate version, at the cost of workspace clutter.

References: Adobe Firefly Boards (a "Vary" action, multi-model side-by-side on one canvas), Krea "Vary" (seed held, perturbed), OpenArt SwitchX, ElevenLabs (each attempt a separate record).

**Decision (§3.4):** a **candidate tray attached to the shot slot** — N variants as thumbnails, one marked selected, the rest retained with their full parameter set. Attached to the *slot*, not a global canvas — that is the documented mitigation for clutter. **[INFER]** The differentiating feature nobody does well is previewing a variant **in situ, with its neighbours**, rather than in isolation; the slot model makes that nearly free.

### 2.6 Progress for 30s–5min jobs

Universal shape across ComfyUI, Runway, and every hosted API: **submit → id → poll/subscribe → terminal state.**

Field rules: never block the UI; place a "generating" card **in the destination slot** so the sequence stays legible; **distinguish queue wait from execution** (different ETAs, different remedies); real percentages, not spinners; **stream preview frames** where available; ETA from throughput; always have a polling fallback (WebSocket drops are common); notify on completion when unfocused.

> **[RESEARCH] A genuine advantage of the local path:** ComfyUI emits **binary preview frames** over its WebSocket — a decoding latent thumbnail. Hosted APIs cannot offer this. It is the single most reassuring progress affordance available, and we get it for free by going local.

## 3. Decisions

### D-1 — The editor is shot-slots over a reduced timeline. Not a node graph, not chat.

**[RESEARCH-backed]** §2.2. Node graphs lost for assembly; chat lost outright; the reduced timeline won.

**Rejected:** a node-graph editor (loses assembly, and ComfyUI already *is* the node graph — duplicating it inside our app is the worst of both); a full multi-track NLE (Resolve exists and is free; we would lose on depth and gain nothing); chat-first assembly (no serious tool does this).

### D-2 — A timeline clip references a shot SLOT, never a file path.

**[RESEARCH-backed]** §2.4. This is the decision that makes non-destructive re-rolls, variant history, provenance, and the render cache all fall out of one indirection. **The most important structural decision in this document.**

### D-3 — Generation backends are pluggable adapters. ComfyUI is the first, not the only.

**[RESEARCH-backed]** §2.1 — Sora's shutdown is proof that vendor permanence is an illusion. One `GenerationBackend` trait; ComfyUI (local) first; hosted adapters (Runway, Luma, Kling) later behind the same seam.

### D-4 — FFmpeg stays a subprocess. Normalize-then-copy-join, two stages.

**Binding: ADR-009.** [RESEARCH] confirms this is also the *technically correct* choice — see §5. Exactly one re-encode generation, which is the minimum possible with heterogeneous AI sources.

### D-5 — Integer frames + explicit rational rate. Never float seconds.

**[RESEARCH-backed]** §4. One of three genuinely irreversible model decisions.

### D-6 — Transitions are zero-duration objects at cut points. Never track overlaps.

**[RESEARCH-backed]** The cautionary tale is concrete: MLT transitions can only exist between two tracks, so when Kdenlive added same-track transitions it had to represent **every visible track as a tractor containing two alternating playlists**. That is the precise architectural tax of getting this wrong.

### D-7 — The job/progress abstraction is built ONCE, generically, before any feature uses it.

**[INFER]** Nothing exists today (§1.1). Both ffmpeg renders and ComfyUI generations are submit→poll→terminal with percentage progress. Building two bespoke mechanisms would be the fourth and fifth ad-hoc progress systems in this codebase. One `Job` abstraction, two producers.

### D-8 — The normalized intermediate IS the preview proxy.

**[RESEARCH-backed]** H.264 / CRF 16 / yuv420p / MP4 is the only profile where concat-demuxer stream-copy is rock solid *and* the file plays natively in a WebView2 `<video>`. One artifact serves both scrubbing and export. **[INFER]** This is a real win specific to our Tauri/WebView2 stack.

### D-9 — ViziWiz's ink editor is kept, not extended.

**[INFER]** It does one thing correctly (annotate a still, flatten, file through the inbox law) and its data model (raster strokes over one image) has nothing to contribute to video assembly. It becomes one *operation* available on an image artifact, not the editor's foundation. **Not deleted** — it works, and the never-delete disposition applies.

## 4. The data model

**[RESEARCH-backed]** Derived from OTIO v0.18.1, FCPXML 1.14, MLT, Remotion, Shotstack, Creatomate — taking what the serious formats agree on and what the SaaS APIs got wrong.

**Three decisions are irreversible. Everything else is additive.**

1. **Integer frames + explicit rational rate** — never float seconds
2. **Stable string IDs on everything**
3. **Transition as a zero-duration object at a cut point** — never a track overlap

```ts
type Frames = number & { readonly __brand: "frames" }; // project-rate frames
type Rate = { num: number; den: number };              // 30000/1001 — never 29.97

type Project = {
  id: string; schemaVersion: 1;
  rate: Rate; sampleRate: number;
  assets: Record<string, Asset>;
  shots: Record<string, ShotSlot>;      // D-2 — the indirection
  sequences: Record<string, Sequence>;  // flat map, NOT one inline timeline
  rootSequenceId: string;
};

/** D-2: a shot is a SLOT with identity; generations are candidates that fill it. */
type ShotSlot = {
  id: string; name: string;
  intent: string;                       // the shot's description — survives every re-roll
  candidates: Candidate[];              // ordered, append-only; never overwritten
  selectedCandidateId: string | null;
};

type Candidate = {
  id: string; assetId: string | null;   // null while generating
  backend: string;                      // "comfyui" | "runway" | … (D-3)
  params: {
    prompt?: string; negativePrompt?: string; seed?: number;
    model?: string; workflowId?: string; workflowHash?: string;
    [k: string]: unknown;
  };
  jobId: string | null;
  createdAt: string;
  status: "pending" | "running" | "ready" | "failed";
  error?: string;
};

type Asset =
  | { id: string; kind: "file"; path: string; nativeRate: Rate;
      availableStart: Frames; availableDuration: Frames;  // MEASURED by ffprobe, never claimed
      width?: number; height?: number; audioChannels?: number;
      fingerprint: string;               // §5.3 — copy-join safety
      probedAt: string }
  | { id: string; kind: "generated"; candidateId: string; materializedAssetId?: string };

type Sequence = { id: string; name: string; rate: Rate; tracks: Track[] };
type Track = { id: string; kind: "video" | "audio"; name: string;
               enabled: boolean; locked: boolean; items: Item[] }; // index = z-order

type Item = Clip | Gap | Transition;    // discriminated union from day one

type Clip = {
  type: "clip"; id: string;
  shotId: string | null;                // D-2 — the slot, when this clip came from one
  assetId: string;                      // resolved from the slot's selected candidate
  timelineStart: Frames; timelineDuration: Frames;  // record in + length
  sourceStart: Frames;  sourceDuration: Frames;     // source in + length (enables §2.4 pattern 2)
  linkGroupId: string | null;           // A/V link — free now, a rewrite later
  enabled: boolean;
  effects: Effect[];                    // ordered; each { id, type, enabled, params }
  gain?: number; pan?: number;
  metadata?: Record<string, unknown>;   // escape hatch, always
};

type Gap = { type: "gap"; id: string; timelineStart: Frames; timelineDuration: Frames };

type Transition = {                     // D-6 — zero timeline length
  type: "transition"; id: string; afterItemId: string;
  kind: "dissolve" | "fade" | string;
  inOffset: Frames; outOffset: Frames; enabled: boolean;
};
```

**Why the non-obvious choices** [RESEARCH]:

- **Brand `Frames`.** At 29.97, one frame = 0.0333666…s. Store `1.7017017017017018` and after a few splits cuts drift: abutting clips overlap by 1e-15 or leave a one-frame black flash, and the renderer rounds differently than the UI. Branding stops a raw seconds value being assigned into a frame field. Convert to seconds in **exactly one place** — the ffmpeg boundary.
- **`sourceDuration` and `timelineDuration` stored separately**, `speed` derived. OTIO-compatible (`LinearTimeWarp.time_scalar`), it is what ffmpeg wants (`setpts=PTS/speed`, `atempo`), reversal is negative, and speed *ramps* later become a keyframed effect rather than a schema change.
- **`linkGroupId` on day one.** Attached A/V is what users expect; separate tracks is what music/VO/J-cuts require. Every real NLE ends with both.
- **`sequences` as a flat map + `rootSequenceId`.** Makes compound clips, multiple sequences, and "the AI proposed an alternate cut" cheap later.
- **`enabled` on effects from the start** — OTIO only added it in 0.18 and it is the most-requested field.
- **Half-open intervals `[start, start+duration)`, always.** EDL and MLT use inclusive out-points and pay for it with permanent off-by-one bugs.

**Validator invariants, from commit one:** items on a track never overlap; `timelineStart` sorted; transition offsets ≤ neighbouring durations; `sourceStart + sourceDuration ≤ asset.availableStart + availableDuration`; all ids unique. `schemaVersion` in the file, migration function written before it is needed.

> **[RESEARCH] The trap specific to AI clips:** generated clips routinely have a *different actual duration* than promised, and container metadata lies (VFR, missing duration, wrong fps). Always `ffprobe`; store the **measured** range; make "asset duration changed since last probe" a representable state.

**OTIO interop is deferred, not designed out.** [RESEARCH] There is **no maintained Rust crate and no production JS/WASM binding** for OpenTimelineIO (negative search finding — re-verify before betting on it). The `Item` union maps 1:1 to OTIO composables and `Frames` → `RationalTime`, so export is a pure function whenever we want it — via subprocessed Python `otioconvert`, the same pattern we already accept for ffmpeg.

## 5. The render pipeline

**[RESEARCH-backed]** Two stages: **normalize each clip independently → concat-demuxer stream-copy.** Right for a subprocess architecture because normalizations are independent, parallelizable, individually progress-reportable, and cacheable; the join is I/O-bound and near-instant.

### 5.1 Normalize (per clip, N in parallel, cap ≈ `num_cpus / 2`)

Target profile: **1920×1080 · 30fps · yuv420p · SAR 1:1 · bt709 · H.264 CRF 16 · AAC 48k stereo · timescale 90000 · 2s closed GOP.**

Non-obvious pieces, each earning its place:

- `scale=…:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease` + `pad` — fits Runway 1280×768, Kling 1280×720, Wan 832×480 onto one canvas without distortion.
- **`setsar=1` — never skip.** Mismatched SAR is the top cause of "why is clip 3 stretched"; some ComfyUI/Wan outputs carry non-1:1 SAR.
- `fps=30` filter **and** `-fps_mode cfr` — belt and braces against VFR.
- `format=yuv420p` **last** and explicit — AI pipelines emit yuv444p and 10-bit yuv420p10le.
- `aresample=48000:async=1:first_pts=0` — holds A/V sync, pads the head if audio starts late.
- **`-video_track_timescale 90000`** — differing tbn is a documented cause of "Non-monotonic DTS" at copy-join.
- **Fixed GOP, `scenecut=0`, `keyint = fps × 2`** — guarantees every clip starts on an IDR, which is what makes the join clean.
- **Explicit colour tags** — otherwise the *first* clip's tags win for the whole output and colour shifts at the cut.
- **No audio track?** Synthesize with `anullsrc` at the probed duration. `-map 0:a?` is **not** a fix — it prevents the error and produces a stream-count mismatch.

**Cache intermediates keyed by `(source hash + profile hash)`.** [INFER] This is what makes re-rolling one shot in a 40-shot sequence nearly free, and it is the direct payoff of D-2.

### 5.2 Join

```
ffmpeg -y -nostdin -f concat -safe 0 -i list.txt \
  -c copy -fflags +genpts -avoid_negative_ts make_zero -max_interleave_delta 0 \
  -movflags +faststart out.mp4
```

The concat list must be UTF-8 **without BOM** (a BOM breaks line-1 detection); `-safe 0` is mandatory for absolute paths.

### 5.3 The safety rule that must not be skipped

> **[RESEARCH] `-c copy` does not error on mismatch.** It copies packets, writes the first input's headers, and **exits 0**. The result plays clip 1 and then breaks. MP4 keeps parameter sets out-of-band in `avcC`, and only the *first* input's SPS/PPS reach the output — so clips from different encoders (Runway vs ComfyUI's ffmpeg vs Kling) decode as garbage from clip 2 onward at identical resolution.

**Therefore, mandatory:**

1. Per-clip **fingerprint** — `(codec, profile, level, width, height, pix_fmt, sar, time_base, color_primaries, color_trc, colorspace, audio_codec, sample_rate, channels, channel_layout)` plus a hash of raw `extradata`/`avcC`. Copy-join **only** when every fingerprint is byte-identical.
2. **ffprobe the output** and compare duration/frame count against the expected sum before reporting success.

**The editor must never trust ffmpeg's exit code for a concat.** This is the video-pipeline twin of the lesson already learned twice in this repo — a green check that verified the wrong thing.

### 5.4 Progress

`-progress pipe:1 -nostats -loglevel error -stats_period 0.2`, stdout for progress, stderr kept for real log lines.

> **[RESEARCH] Use `out_time_us`, never `out_time_ms`.** Current ffmpeg master emits the same microsecond `pts` for both keys. Trac #7345 (filed 2018) is still unfixed and will stay unfixed because fixing it breaks every downstream parser.

Percentage: get `format.duration` from ffprobe up front, then `(out_time_us / 1e6) / total * 100`, **clamped to [0,100]** (trim/filter ops overshoot). `speed=` gives the ETA. On the **copy-join step `frame=`/`fps=` are absent or zero** — `out_time` is the only usable signal. Parse defensively: values can be `N/A`.

> **[RESEARCH] Rust deadlock warning.** Never `wait()` before draining both pipes — the child blocks on a full 64KB Windows pipe buffer and never exits. Drain stdout and stderr on separate tasks, then wait. Use `CREATE_NO_WINDOW` (0x08000000) or flash a console on every spawn. Pair `Stdio::null()` with `-nostdin`.
>
> **[GROUND] And kill the process TREE**, not the child — this repo already learned that the hard way (`scripts/lib/kill-tree.mjs`, commit 76223b9, 7 leaked processes per run).

## 6. ComfyUI integration

**[RESEARCH]** Verified against ComfyUI `master`, July 2026. Default port 8188 (desktop app uses 8000). Repo is now `Comfy-Org/ComfyUI`.

### 6.1 The contract we bind to

- **Submit:** `POST /api/prompt` with `{prompt: <API-format>, client_id}` → `{prompt_id, number, node_errors}`.
- **Jobs API (v0.26.0+):** `GET /api/jobs`, `GET /api/jobs/{id}`, `POST /api/jobs/{id}/cancel` — a normalised layer over queue+history. **Prefer it** to reconciling `/queue` and `/history` by hand.
- **WebSocket:** `ws://127.0.0.1:8188/ws?clientId=<uuid>`. **Generate the clientId ourselves and pass the same value as `client_id` in the POST**, or we get broadcast-only messages with no targeted `executed` payloads.
- **Progress:** build the bar on **`progress_state`** (per-node `{value, max, state}`), not the legacy `progress`.
- **Preview frames:** binary frames, event `1` (PREVIEW_IMAGE) / `4` (PREVIEW_IMAGE_WITH_METADATA). Send the `feature_flags` handshake first to get the metadata-bearing form. **This is our live decode thumbnail** (§2.6).

> **[RESEARCH] Completion gotcha:** do **not** use `queue_remaining == 0`. Since ~Aug 2025 `progress_state` keeps emitting all-`finished` frames for 20–30s after the run and the `status` frame is delayed (issue #9330). Key completion off `execution_success` / `execution_error` / `execution_interrupted` matched on `prompt_id`, then read `/api/jobs/{id}`.

### 6.2 Workflow format and parameterisation

**API format only.** The UI format (`File → Save`) is not submittable, and **there is still no programmatic UI→API converter in core** (issue #6895) — the real logic lives in frontend TypeScript. The official "strip x/y, drop groups" advice is oversimplified and misleading: it does not resolve links, reroutes, primitives, muted/bypassed nodes, or subgraphs.

**Decision: ship hand-exported API-format workflows as bundled assets. Never convert at runtime.**

**Parameterise by `_meta.title`.** Nodes are renamed in the ComfyUI UI to stable handles (`POSITIVE`, `NEGATIVE`, `SEED`, `INPUT_IMAGE`); we look up by title and **hard-error if a required title is missing.** Lookup by `class_type` breaks with two text encoders; lookup by node id breaks on re-export.

> **[RESEARCH] There is no API stability guarantee** — not for the HTTP/WS surface, not for the workflow API format. Issue #11833 is an open complaint that updates break API-format workflows when nodes gain new required-with-default inputs.
>
> **Mitigation, mandatory:** pin an exact ComfyUI version alongside bundled workflows, and **validate every workflow against `/object_info` at startup**, surfacing a clear "this workflow needs updating for ComfyUI vX" rather than failing mid-generation.

### 6.3 Where the HTTP client lives

**[INFER]** No HTTP client exists in Rust today (§1.1). Two options:

- **(a) Rust-side** — add `reqwest` + a WS client; the seam stays `*.native.ts` → Rust command, matching `patterns.md` shape 5 exactly.
- **(b) Renderer-side** — `fetch`/`WebSocket` straight from TS to `127.0.0.1:8188`.

**Recommend (a).** It keeps the established native-seam pattern intact, keeps ComfyUI's address out of the renderer, allows spawning/supervising a local ComfyUI as a sidecar later, and puts binary preview-frame decoding on a thread rather than the UI. **[INFER]** (b) is faster to build and genuinely tempting for a local-only service — worth a spike if (a) proves heavy, but the pattern cost is real.

`rucomfyui` (v1.1.1, 2026-07-02) is a maintained Rust client that codegens typed node structs from `/object_info` — **worth evaluating**, not adopting sight-unseen.

## 8. Phasing

Each phase is a testable exe. No phase depends on a later one.

| Phase | Delivers | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| **E1 — Spine** | `domains/editor`, the §4 model + validator (unit-tested, no UI), `jobs.rs` (D-7), `dpl:job`, ffprobe → fingerprint + measured range | Model validator green; a probed asset shows its **measured** duration; a fake job reports 0→100% |
| **E2 — Assemble + render** | Timeline UI (single video track: order, trim, gap), pick clips from the content library, normalize→join with cache, verify-after-render | **N AI clips of differing resolution/fps/codec → one correct mp4**, byte-verified duration, live percentage |
| **E3 — Shot slots + bake-off** | Shot slots (D-2), candidate tray, in-situ variant preview, non-destructive swap | Re-roll a shot mid-sequence; neighbours untouched; previous candidates retained and switchable |
| **E4 — ComfyUI** | `comfy.rs`, workflow assets + `_meta.title` parameterisation, `/object_info` validation, live preview frames | A bundled workflow generates into a slot with a decoding thumbnail and a real progress bar |
| **E5 — Transitions + audio** | `xfade`/`acrossfade` (accepting the second encode), one audio bed | A dissolve renders correctly; transitions still zero-duration in the model |

**E1 and E2 are the load-bearing pass.** [INFER] If the model and render pipeline are right, everything after is additive; if they are wrong, everything after is rework. E4 is deliberately *not* first despite being the most exciting — it depends on the job abstraction and the slot model, and building it first would produce both in throwaway form.

## 9. Boundaries

- 🚫 Never a crate-linked ffmpeg (**ADR-009**). Subprocess only.
- 🚫 Never mutate a source artifact. Renders are new artifacts; the inbox/content laws hold.
- 🚫 Never point a timeline clip at a file path (**D-2**).
- 🚫 Never trust `-c copy`'s exit code (**§5.3**).
- 🚫 Never hand-edit `routeTree.gen.ts` or ColdLight `generated/*.css`.
- ⚠️ Ask before: adding a Rust HTTP dependency (§6.3), bundling or auto-installing ComfyUI, or any outbound network call to a **hosted** service. Local `127.0.0.1` ComfyUI is in scope; hosted vendors are a separate decision with its own credential story.
- **Out of scope:** multi-track audio mixing, colour grading, keyframed effects, OTIO/FCPXML export, hosted generation vendors, P2P share (owned by `capture-share-spec.md`).

## 11. What I am least sure of

Stated plainly, because this document becomes canonical:

1. **§2.3's framing** — that this is primarily an iteration-management UX — is the inference the whole design leans on. It comes from credit-metered SaaS reporting; your GPU-minutes economics are different in kind, even if the structural argument survives.
2. **§6.3's recommendation** (HTTP in Rust) is a pattern-consistency argument, not a performance one. For a purely local service, renderer-side `fetch` is defensible and cheaper.
3. **The OTIO binding gap is a negative search finding.** Re-verify crates.io and npm directly before relying on it.
4. **Sections 1–2 rest substantially on trade press**, because product interaction models are rarely documented primarily. The ComfyUI, ffmpeg, and timeline-format sections (3–5) are grounded in primary sources and are much more reliable.
5. **Phasing assumes E1/E2 are load-bearing.** If you would rather see ComfyUI generating something on screen sooner — for motivation or validation — that is a legitimate reordering, at the cost of throwaway job/slot scaffolding.
